I. PURPOSE 
 
Establish the training, use, deployment, and reporting guidelines for the Department First Aid, 
Automated External Defibrillator (AED), Overdose Intervention Drug (OID), and Combat 
Application Tourniquet (CAT) Programs. 
 
II. POLICY 
 
The Department’s First Aid Training Program shall be certified by the American Red Cross with all 
enforcement personnel being certified in Adult and Pediatric First Aid / CPR / AED, unless an 
exception has been made by the Superintendent. 
 
Department AEDs shall be placed in the workplace in compliance with all federal and state 
regulations.  
 
The Department’s Overdose Intervention Drug Program authority shall be supported by an Indiana 
State Police Naloxone Standing Order issued biennially by the Department’s consulting physician. 
Only Department employees who have successfully completed the OID training (in compliance with 
all federal and state regulations) are authorized to administer the intervention drugs as described in 
the Naloxone Standing Order. 
 
The Department shall provide biennial first aid training instruction in all aspects of the First Aid 
Training program in the odd numbered years, regardless of when an employee was initially certified. 
All applicable First Aid-related training hours shall be documented in Acadis. 
 
All non-training uses of an AED, OID, or CAT shall be properly documented as outlined in each 
section below and shall be reported to the Tactical Training Coordinator through channels on Blue 
Teams.  The initial report should be submitted within 24 hours of use. 
 
III. PROCEDURE 
 
A. The Department’s First Aid Program shall: 
 
1. Be certified by the American Red Cross with all enforcement personnel being certified in Adult 
and Pediatric First Aid / CPR / AED, unless the Superintendent has exempted the personnel; 
 
2. Include initial instruction and biennial re-training (in the odd numbered years) in first aid, AED, 
OID, CAT and any other pertinent or related topics; 
 
3. Authorize the Department’s consulting physician to direct and control the medical aspects of the 
AED and OID Programs.

B. FIRST AID PROGRAM 
 
1. First Aid Instructors 
 
a. District responsibilities: 
 
(i) Maintain at least one (1) American Red Cross First Aid instructor; 
(ii) Select personnel who are willing to become instructors and have an ability to teach; 
(iii) Provide time for personnel to attend the training courses for instructors; and 
(iv) Provide a minimum of three (3) hours biennially (occurring every two-years) training and 
instructional materials. 
 
b. Instructor training: 
 
(i) Instructors shall be certified by a designated Department American Red Cross instructor; 
(ii) Department American Red Cross instructors shall be designated by the Training Division; and 
(iii) Shall adhere to the re-certification guidelines established by the American Red Cross. 
 
c. Instructor responsibilities: 
 
(i). Conduct an American Red Cross First Aid recertification training biennially (during odd 
numbered years)  
(ii) Utilize the chain of command to assist with the administration of American Red Cross First Aid 
training; 
(iii) Provide necessary information to the employees to have all training hours entered into 
ACADIS; and 
(iv) American Red Cross First Aid instructors are prohibited from using their Department American 
Red Cross instructor certification to instruct outside individuals or agencies. Outside requests for 
training shall be directed to contact the American Red Cross Training Center in their area. 
 
d. Training Division responsibilities: 
 
(i) Monitor the progress of the American Red Cross First Aid program; 
(ii) Conduct American Red Cross First Aid training for headquarters personnel and other units as 
necessary; 
(iii) Provide the written and skills tests for the training course to all districts; and 
(iv) Forward student certification cards to the appropriate instructors for distribution. Only regional 
dispatch personnel, CPS officers and personnel who “must prove first-aid certification” shall be 
issued certification cards. 
 
e. All enforcement personnel are required to: 
 
(i) Be certified in a 16-hour American Red Cross First Aid training course during their basic training 
school; and 
(ii) Biennially (in odd numbered years) attend an American Red Cross First Aid re-certification 
training course online or at the individual’s assigned district,    section, division, or at GHQ. 
Personnel shall attend the scheduled biennial re-certification regardless of their initial certification 
date.

C. Automated External Defibrillator (AED) Program 
 
1. The Commander of the Training Division shall assign the Tactical Training Coordinator in the 
Training Division to serve as the Automated External Defibrillator Program (AED) Coordinator. 
The AED Program Coordinator (AED-PC) shall: 
 
a. Be responsible for overseeing and monitoring the program; 
 
b. Ensure compliance with all federal and state regulations on public access defibrillation, the 
federal Cardiac Arrest Survival Act, and Indiana Code 16-31-6.5; and 
 
c. Shall notify the EMS Commission of the acquisition and location of AED equipment per Indiana 
Code 16-31-6.5-5(2). Department personnel shall not place or move any AED without approval by 
the AED-PC. 
 
2. District or division CPR instructors will assist the AED-PC by monitoring the AEDs located at 
district facilities or divisions throughout the state. They will be responsible for maintaining and 
testing of the AEDs according to the manufacturer’s recommendations. District or division CPR 
instructors shall notify the AED-PC each time the AED has been used. 
 
3. A Blue Team report titled “First Aid Deployment” shall be completed each time the AED has 
been attached to a victim, constituting an “AED Use” within 24 hrs of the event. 
 
4. The Department’s selection of AED equipment shall comply with the recommendations of the 
American Red Cross’ current guidelines. 
 
5. Each AED location shall also have other medical equipment and supplies to support the safe and 
complete management of workplace cardiac emergencies, such as: blood borne pathogens responder 
and clean-up kits; CPR barrier masks; and latex/vinyl gloves. 
 
6. Records of all AED-related training shall be documented in ACADIS. 
 
7. Records of all AED locations, services and updates shall be maintained in the Training Division. 
 
D. Department Overdose Intervention Drug (OID) Program 
 
1. The commander of the Training Division will assign the Tactical Training Coordinator as the 
Department’s Overdose Intervention Drug Program Coordinator. The OID Program Coordinator 
(OID-PC) shall: 
 
a. Be responsible for overseeing and monitoring the program; 
  
4  
b. Ensure compliance with all federal and state regulations by maintaining a copy of the Indiana 
State Police Standing Order on Naloxone, ensuring proper notification of the State Department of 
Health and compliance with other applicable portions of Indiana Code 16-31 and IC 16-42-27; and 
 
c. Department personnel shall not transfer intervention equipment from one employee to another, 
unless in an emergency situation for administration, without prior authorization of the OID-PC or a 
designee. 
 
2. The OID will be part of the Department’s First Aid Training Program with employees retraining 
in the OID program during First Aid Training. 
 
3. Each Department First Aid instructor shall become and maintain instructor status for the 
application of Overdose Intervention Drug techniques. Instructors will assist the OID-PC by 
monitoring the location of the intervention equipment issued in the field in their areas of 
responsibility. 
 
4. OID instructors are prohibited from using their Department OID instructor status or training to 
instruct outside individuals or agencies. Outside requests for training shall be directed to contact the 
Training Division commander. 
 
5. A Blue Team Report titled “First Aid Deployment” shall be completed each time intervention 
equipment has been administered. An “OID use” only occurs when intervention equipment has been 
administered either partially, or  fully, to a potential overdose victim. 
 
6. When administered to a potential overdose victim, all OID (used and partially used) containers 
shall be provided to EMS personnel for delivery to the attending physician. 
 
7. The officer utilizing the overdose intervention equipment shall complete a new First Aid 
Deployment Report in Blue Team within 24 hours of each use of the OID. 
 
8. The Department’s selection of narcotic overdose equipment shall comply with the Indiana State 
Police Standing Order on Naloxone issued by the Department consulting physician biennially. 
 
9. Each employee trained in OID shall also have other medical equipment and supplies to support 
the safe and complete management of narcotic overdose emergencies, such as: blood borne 
pathogens responder and clean-up kits; CPR barrier masks; and latex/vinyl gloves. 
 
10. Each employee is responsible to maintain their issued OID in at  temperature that is compliant 
with the manufacturer’s instructions on the packaging. The employee must notify the OID-PC if 
their issued OID is expired or stored in conditions that are outside the manufacturer’s requirements. 
The employee must request a replacement from Quartermaster. 
 
11. OID Quality Assurance Program. 
 
a. The Department Consulting Physician shall conduct a case-by-case review of each Department 
Overdose Intervention Drug technique administration. 
 
b. Records of all OID-related training shall be maintained in ACADIS.

c. Records of all overdose intervention equipment issued to employees with expiration dates and 
updates shall be maintained by the Quartermaster Section. 
 
E. Department Combat Application Tourniquet (CAT) Program 
 
1. The commander of the Training Division shall assign the Tactical Training coordinator as the 
Department Combat Application Tourniquet (CAT) Program coordinator. The CAT Program 
coordinator (CAT-PC) shall be responsible for overseeing and monitoring the program; 
 
2. The CAT will be part of the Department’s First Aid Training Program with employees retraining 
in the CAT program during First Aid Training. 
 
3. Each Department First Aid instructor shall become and maintain training as an instructor for the 
application of the Combat Application Tourniquet. Instructors shall assist the CAT-PC by 
monitoring the equipment issued in the field in their areas of responsibility. 
 
4. A Blue Team report titled “First Aid Deployment” shall be completed each time a CAT has been 
administered. A “CAT use” only occurs when the CAT equipment has been applied fully to an arm 
or leg of a victim with a bleeding injury. 
 
5. The officer utilizing the CAT equipment shall complete a First Aid Deployment Report in Blue 
Team within 24-hours of each use of the CAT. 
 
6. CAT instructors are prohibited from using their Department CAT instructor status or training to 
instruct outside individuals or agencies. Outside requests for training shall be directed to contact the 
Training Division commander. 
 
7. Records of all CAT-related training shall be maintained in ACADIS.  
 
8. Records of all CAT equipment issued to employees shall be maintained by the Quartermaster 
Section. 
 
F. This procedure is to be used in conjunction with all relevant Department regulations, rules, 
policies, and procedures.
